01

Why design

I spent six years inside financial product before calling myself a designer. I started as a product analyst, looking at metrics and user behavior, and that origin shaped how I design: an interface decision is only finished when I can explain its effect on the business.

At Franq I worked with other people's money: plan purchases, commissions, business-client analysis, registration data. These are flows where a copy mistake or a broken hierarchy costs trust, and sometimes costs compliance. I learned to design with rules, with legal nearby and with a developer beside me.

That is the kind of problem I want to keep solving, at a larger scale: complex financial product, many people involved, decisions that must be defended with data and research. A generalist profile, from discovery to post-release.

Where I fit

  • End-to-end product conception within a business segment or channel
  • Discovery and strategic definition alongside business and product
  • Build, prototyping and release with engineering
  • Tracking outcomes after go-live
  • Reading data and metrics without needing a translator
02

How I work

Five stages. None of them is mine alone, and that is why it works.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    I understand the problem before accepting the request. Desk research, usage data, conversations with client-facing teams and interviews when the open question is behavioral.

    With business, data and support

  2. 02

    Strategic definition

    I turn the finding into a frame: which user, which moment of the journey, which metric moves. This is where most of the scope gets cut.

    With product and leadership

  3. 03

    Build

    Information architecture, flows, high-fidelity prototype on top of the design system. I test with users before handing off to dev.

    With design system and research

  4. 04

    Release

    I follow implementation component by component, review in staging and resolve the edge cases that only surface in code.

    With engineering and QA

  5. 05

    Outcome

    I look at the numbers after go-live and compare them with the hypothesis. If nothing moved, I go back to step 01 with a better question.

    With data and product

How I work in a team

With engineering

I hand off in design-system components, not loose screens. I specify states, errors and data limits, and review the implementation before release.

With product

I take part in prioritization with evidence: what research said, what the data shows, what can be cut without losing the value.

With business and legal

I bring the flow to validation early. Terms, mandatory disclaimers and eligibility rules go into the design, not after it.

With support

I use tickets as a continuous research source: it is where friction shows up first and cheapest.

06

Accessibility and inclusion

A financial product is an essential service: if someone cannot use it, that is a design problem. What I check on every handoff.

Contrast and legibility

Text always above 4.5:1, 16px minimum body size, never color as the only carrier of information.

Keyboard and screen reader

Focus order, labels and described error messages. A financial flow must be completable without a mouse.

Plain language

Inherited from my years in educational design: I rewrite financial jargon until it fits a first-time investor.

Targets and mobile

44px minimum touch area and a small-screen review of the flow, which is where most of the audience is.

Diversity in the sample

Research recruitment varied in age, income and digital familiarity. Advanced users alone hide the problem.
