Commission Management. Seeing the status of every sale without opening a ticket.

app.franq.com.br/meus-ganhos

A Kanban that stopped raising questions and started answering one: what stage is my payment at?

The "My Earnings" screen is where the Personal Banker tracks their entire sales pipeline in a Kanban format, moving through the stages In Process, Invoice, Payment and Completed. The existing layout was dense, hard to scan and didn't communicate the status of each deal well, generating frequent doubts and support tickets.

Problem

The screen was dense, hard to scan and didn’t communicate each deal’s status well. Without knowing what stage a payment was at, PBs turned to support.

Solution

Cards with a clear hierarchy, status badges with semantic colors, filters at the top, an easier statement download and a list view as an alternative to the Kanban.

My role

UX Designer, responsible for analyzing complaints and screen usage, validation wireframes, the final interface, responsive variations and component documentation.

Design process

I started from user complaints and real screen usage before touching the visuals. The work followed four phases: Discover, Define, Design and Deliver.

  1. Discover: gather the main complaints and analyze how filters and card information were used.

    User complaints / Usage analysis

  2. Define: validate the information hierarchy in wireframes before visuals.

    Wireframe / Information hierarchy

  3. Design: semantic status colors, filters, statement action and list view.

    Kanban / List / Status badges

2 views: Kanban + List

Redesigning a payments screen reinforced a few principles:

  1. 1.Status is the main information. On a payments screen, the first question is “what stage is it at?”.
  2. 2.Color always paired with text. The badge has to work for people who can’t tell the colors apart.
  3. 3.Wireframes save visual debates. Validating hierarchy first avoids rework.
  4. 4.Two views, same data. Kanban to follow the flow, list to check amounts.
  5. 5.Support tickets are research. The most frequent questions showed what the screen wasn’t saying.
