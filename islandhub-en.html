From selling in Facebook groups to a single platform, designed for people arriving, staying or leaving the island.

IslandHub’s goal is an intuitive, trustworthy digital marketplace for residents and expats in the Cayman Islands, making it easy to buy and sell new and used products, with a focus on simplicity.

Today most sales on the island happen in messy Facebook groups or by word of mouth. I conceived the product from scratch: scope, personas, benchmarking, architecture, flows, interfaces, design system and navigable prototypes — from AI-assisted listings to stores, promotions and service booking.

Problem

Sales scattered across Facebook groups and word of mouth: unorganized listings, little trust, scams and no-shows. Expats who arrive need to buy fast; those leaving need to sell fast.

Solution

A single marketplace with search and filters by island, AI-assisted listing, chat with offers, reputation, stores for local businesses, promotions and service booking — light enough to work on limited internet.

My role

Freelance UX/UI Designer, end to end: project scope, personas and empathy map, benchmarking, architecture and flows, per-feature specs, web and mobile UI, design system and prototypes for testing.

03

Design

Search and discovery

Search with autosuggest and filters by category, price, condition and location — Grand Cayman, Cayman Brac and Little Cayman — plus Flash Deals and store browsing.

Listing: four ways

“Sell Now” offers four ways to create a listing: manual, step by step; AI assistant, by chat; import from another platform’s link; and bulk import from a spreadsheet, for stores with large inventory. The manual flow covers category, attributes, photos, price, quantity and variants, delivery (pickup, seller delivery or IslandHub Delivery) and free or paid publishing.

AI in listings, with clear limits

In the AI listing proposal, AI is not a parallel path but a layer: it reads the photos and returns a draft the seller only confirms. Three confidence tiers decide how each field behaves:

Auto

confidence ≥ 0.85 · reversible field

Fills in and marks with the AI badge: color, category, item type, material. One tap undoes it.

Suggests

0.50 – 0.85 · or high-impact field

Pre-selects but requires confirmation, with the likeliest alternatives beside it: brand, model, size.

Asks

< 0.50 · or declarative field

Empty field and a direct question: price, condition, authenticity, flaws, battery health.

AI never fills price, condition, authenticity or identifiers such as IMEI or serial number on its own — it suggests, but the statement is the seller’s.

Chat and negotiation

The in-app chat ties buyer and seller to the listing, with quick replies (“Is this still available?”), photo and location sharing, and an offer inside the conversation: the seller accepts, declines or counters.

Buying and orders

From quick view to reporting a listing, from a cart with stock states (out of stock, limited stock) to a declined payment. For sellers, every order has a clear status — awaiting confirmation, preparing, ready for pickup, out for delivery, completed, declined, cancelled or refunded.

Stores, promotions and banners

Local businesses get a store page, with a preview before publishing, sponsored cards and promotions — store-level or global campaigns. Categories got banners and a library of 357 illustrations.

Services and booking

The marketplace also sells services. I designed two models: fixed price, like a salon — the customer picks a service and time and is charged automatically after the appointment — and quote-based, like a garage — the provider confirms the time and sends a quote to approve; if the final amount changes, the customer approves it before the charge.

04

Deliver

Prototypes to test with people

The selling flow became a navigable prototype with three scenarios — an iPhone, a pair of jeans and a sofa — and presenter notes. Booking became a prototype to test with two people at once, customer and provider: everything is connected, with guided test scenarios and a feedback form on every screen.

Design system

A documented design system underpins everything: layered tokens — primitive, semantic and component —, typography, scales, marketplace components and order-status badges, with mandatory contrast fixes, visible focus on every component and a shadcn/ui base for implementation.

Indicators the project should move

The indicators below come from the AI listing proposal and turn the goal of listing fast — without losing quality — into measurable signals.

  • Time to publishFrom first photo to live listing — target under 90 s on the happy path.
  • Accepted without edits% of AI-filled fields the seller doesn’t change, per field and category.
  • Attribute coverage% of listings with every filterable field — what makes search work.
  • Drop-off per stepWhere the listing funnel leaks, compared with the manual flow.
  • Listings per sellerIf listing got easy, the second and third listings come faster.
  • Description disputesCounter-metric: if AI writes too much, this goes up.

Outcome

A product designed from scratch — and what it taught meDraft · review

IslandHub went from scope to a complete product, organized into four main areas — access and marketplace, profile, listing and promotion, stores and services —, with about 1,600 screens, per-feature specs, navigable prototypes and a documented design system.

Designing an entire marketplace for such a specific context reinforced a few principles:

  1. 1.The persona defines the MVP. Starting with Jessa put the fast-selling flow ahead of everything else.
  2. 2.AI is a layer, not a path. It speeds up filling in, but never makes a statement on the seller’s behalf.
  3. 3.Trust is designed. Reputation, chat with offers, verification and reporting replace “will they show up?”.
  4. 4.Local context changes the product. Three islands, district instead of ZIP code, KYD, limited internet and island delivery.
  5. 5.Specify states before the final screen. Errors, loading and acceptance criteria defined early avoid rework in development.
